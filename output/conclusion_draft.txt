DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
=================================================================

We presented SV-Flow, a lightweight inference-time diversity enhancement
framework that decouples Stein Variational Gradient Descent (SVGD) repulsive
forces into the center-of-mass space (R^3) of each ligand, while preserving
chemical integrity through tangent-plane projection and time-annealed
scheduling.

On the CrossDocked test set (N=100 pockets, 10 trajectories per pocket),
SV-Flow Core achieves a mean Tanimoto chemical diversity of 0.882
(vs. 0.848 for DrugFlow independent sampling), representing a
4.1% relative improvement (paired t-test: t(99)=4.35,
p=3.36e-05, Cohen's d=0.435). After light MMFF94
energy minimization (median RMSD 0.722 A, <5% of
molecules exceeding 2.0 A), internal steric clashes are eliminated in both
methods (0.00 clashes/mol), and chemical identity markers (QED, SA score)
remain unchanged, confirming that the diversity gain is not an artifact of
strained geometries.

Notably, SV-Flow Core with N=10 trajectories approaches the diversity of
MaxMin post-hoc selection from a 5x larger sampling pool (N=50 -> 10;
Tanimoto diversity 0.902), demonstrating
that coupled SVGD repulsion during sampling is fundamentally more efficient
than post-hoc filtering. The centroid spatial variance increases from
0.108 to 0.135 (+24.6%
relative), indicating that SV-Flow molecules explore more diverse binding
modes within the pocket.

The QED drug-likeness exhibits a modest trade-off (0.504 vs.
0.547 for DrugFlow, p=2.41e-04), consistent with the
expected quality-diversity Pareto frontier: molecules that are more
structurally diverse tend to have slightly lower drug-likeness scores.
Post-minimization bond anomaly rates (0.025
vs. 0.017) and broken ring fractions
(0.006 vs. 0.000)
remain within acceptable ranges for both methods.

A limitation of the current study is its restriction to the CrossDocked
benchmark; future work should validate these findings on more diverse pocket
geometries (e.g., PDBbind, DUD-E) and with different base generative models
(e.g., TargetDiff, DiffSBDD). Additionally, while MMFF94 minimization is
computationally lightweight and effectively resolves internal clashes, a
full protein-ligand complex minimization with explicit solvent may further
improve physical realism.

In summary, SV-Flow provides a simple, principled, and computationally
inexpensive approach to enhancing binding mode diversity at inference time,
requiring no retraining and adding minimal overhead (~0.2 s/molecule for
SVGD repulsion). The framework is model-agnostic and can be readily applied
to any flow-matching or diffusion-based SBDD pipeline.
